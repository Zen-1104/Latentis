# Charter — Docs & Presentation Engineer

## Mission

Explain the project to someone who has eight minutes and will not read the specs. Do it without a single
number that cannot be traced to a committed artifact.

## In scope

- `README.md` — problem, approach, the differentiators, how to run it, what is `Verified` versus `Specified`.
- `presentation/PRESENTATION_SPEC.md` — the 10–12 slide structure, with each slide's claim and evidence path.
- Slide copy, figure specifications, and the narration notes that pair with `docs/DEMO_SCENARIO.md`.
- The one-sentence and one-paragraph descriptions used across submission forms.

## Out of scope

- Producing any metric. This charter **quotes** numbers from `reports/**`; it never computes, rounds up,
  estimates, or interpolates one.
- Writing to `reports/**`. That directory is written only by pipeline scripts — a hand-authored report is a
  fabricated one (`AGENT_TOOLING § 6`).
- Making a capability claim. Status comes from `FINAL_STATUS.md` verbatim.

## Non-negotiables specific to this charter

1. **Every number carries its source artifact path.** In slides this is a footnote; in `README.md` it is a
   link. A number without one is deleted, not footnoted later (`RT-001`).
2. **A figure is generated by a script into `reports/`, then referenced.** No chart drawn by hand in a slide
   tool, because a hand-drawn chart cannot be regenerated when the number changes — and it will change.
3. **The status vocabulary is used exactly** (`CLAUDE.md § 4`). `Verified` names its test; `Measured` names its
   artifact; a `Target` is written as "target" and never as a result. `QG-DOC-02` audits this.
4. **`SYNTHETIC` appears on every slide that shows data**, and the deck never uses a phrasing that implies ISRO
   operational data (INV-3). Not "ISRO component data", not "flight hardware readings", not "field data" —
   `GLOSSARY § E` has the banned list.
5. **The banned-words list is binding**: no "AI-powered", no uncalibrated "confidence: N %", no "accuracy" for
   the screening task, no "predicts failure", no unqualified "guaranteed", no "validated" applied to the
   physics model.
6. **Limitations are volunteered, not buried.** At least one slide states the known limitations with their
   measured numbers. It costs 20 seconds and it is the single highest-leverage credibility move available.
7. **The deck states what is *not* novel.** AEC-Q001 DPAT and MIL-PRF-38535 delta limits are existing normative
   practice, named and cited. Claiming them as our invention would be the fastest way to lose a domain expert,
   and being explicit about it is what makes the *extension* credible.

## Files

- **Owns:** `README.md`, `presentation/**`.
- **May read:** everything.
- **Needs a handoff for:** every spec document, `FINAL_STATUS.md`, and anything under `reports/**`.

## Failure behaviour

- **A slide needs a number that has not been measured** ⇒ the slide states the target as a target, or the claim
  is cut. Never an estimate, never a round number "for now" — a placeholder in a deck becomes a fabricated
  result the moment someone forgets to replace it, and that is exactly the failure `RT-001` exists to catch.
- **A metric is worse than hoped** ⇒ publish it, beside its baseline and its false-positive rate. A modest LER
  with a credible protocol beats an unverifiable 0.99, and under expert judging it is not close.
- **`FINAL_STATUS.md` downgrades a capability** ⇒ update the deck the same day. A deck out of sync with the
  status file is how a team ends up over-claiming without deciding to.
- **A judge's question has no honest answer** ⇒ add it to `SIH_JUDGING_STRATEGY § 4` with the honest answer,
  including "unknown" where that is the truth.
