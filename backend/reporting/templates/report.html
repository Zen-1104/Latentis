What this report says

This is a record of one decision about one part, and of the evidence behind it. Two independent checks were applied: whether the part meets the fixed limit that every part must meet, and whether it resembles the other parts made alongside it. A part can pass the first and still fail the second — finding those is the purpose of this tool.

How to read the terms used above

TermWhat it means here
Fixed limit A threshold every part must meet, independent of any other part. Conventional screening uses only this.
Peer comparison
(Dynamic PAT)		 The same reading judged against the other parts from the same lot, rather than against a fixed number. This is what catches a part that is within spec but unlike its neighbours.
Robust distance How far this reading sits from the middle of its lot, measured in units of the lot's own typical spread. Larger means more unusual. It is computed with a median-based spread so a handful of extreme parts cannot mask the rest.
Prediction range
(conformal bound)		 A calibrated range for where a future reading is expected to fall — never a guarantee. It is built so that, across many parts, the true value lands inside it about as often as the configured level says it should. A wide range means the evidence is thin, and that is reported rather than hidden.
Horizon How far ahead the projection is carried. Figures labelled 168 h are projections to that point, not measurements.
Risk index A single ordering number combining the checks above, used only to put a work queue in a sensible order. It is not a probability of failure.
